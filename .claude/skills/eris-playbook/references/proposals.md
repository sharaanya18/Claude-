**E1 (2026-10-02) AI baseline looks like a single-hold-out, no-ensemble agent pipeline.** AIDE's prompts require a first draft "relatively simple, without ensembling or hyper-parameter optimization", one atomic improvement per step, a printed hold-out metric, K-fold only "if appropriate", greedy best-node selection (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WecoAI/aideml/main/aide/agent.py; https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13138). o1-preview+AIDE: 29.4% above median, 16.9% medal on MLE-bench (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07095v5); newer agents reach 35-64% on MLE-bench(-Lite) but only 7-13% on live Kaggle (KompeteAI Table 4, https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10177). Use: beat the printed baseline with shift-aware grouped CV and a diverse refit ensemble rather than more tuning of one recipe. Converges with platform-facts section 6.4. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.

**E2 (2026-10-02) Winner's curse in selection: quantified.** Picking the max validation node costs 9.4-16.6 pp of medal rate versus oracle selection (AIRA-dojo, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554); AIRA2 attributes most of it to evaluation noise (bugs, stochastic splits, brittle metric parsing; https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499v2); HPO overtuning grows with hold-out validation, small data, many trials (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19540). Use: repeated grouped CV, small fixed search, different folds for search and selection, ship an average of the top-k configs rather than the argmax (the top-k averaging for a single submission is untested here). Converges with validation-recipes V3/V4/V9. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.

**E3 (2026-10-02) Agent code leaks via preprocessing and mishandles time.** Test-statistic preprocessing raised validation 0.8188 to 0.8677 while test fell 0.8033 to 0.7343 (MLE-STAR Table 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15692v2); AssistedDS found datetime columns dropped or treated as categories and train/test feature-drop mismatches (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13992); models adopted misleading hints up to 97.75% of the time. Use: encode time/entity structure explicitly, fit every transform on train only, test any description hint under grouped CV before trusting it. Converges with CLAUDE.md 2.3 #5 and validation-recipes V12. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.

**E4 (2026-10-02) Shortcut taking by agents; creators may plant traps.** BaitBench: 57.1% of frontier-agent runs exploited planted entity-overlap, near-duplicate or no-signal shortcuts; hacked runs had a median public-to-held-out gap of 0.25 accuracy; 92.4% recognised the exploit and shipped it (https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30724). Use: audit train for entity/near-duplicate lookup structure, run a shuffled-label CV (must be chance), build entity-disjoint CV; an agent baseline that fell for a lookup is easier to beat on the hidden split than its public score implies. Converges with 00-s-tier anti-pattern "near-perfect score on a noisy task is a leakage tell" and V12. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.

**E5 (2026-10-02) Hidden-shift units and what predicts the gap.** Designers use time, entity, scaffold/homology cluster, domain, subpopulation and label-prevalence shift; ID-to-OOD drops of about 20% (WILDS https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07421, Wild-Time https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14238); TableShift: shift gap tracks label-distribution shift (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07577); accuracy-on-the-line with pretrained-vs-scratch exceptions (https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04649); DomainBed: tuned ERM plus an honest model-selection rule beats most robust algorithms (https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01434). Use: mirror the unit, add a label-prevalence-by-group diagnostic, prefer a strong ERM/pretrained representation selected on grouped OOF. Converges with V1/V2/V5/V7; the prevalence diagnostic is new. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.

**E6 (2026-10-02) Multi-term metrics reward the weakest slice and calibrated uncertainty under shift.** Jigsaw uses a power mean with p=-5 over subgroup AUCs plus overall AUC (Borkan et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04561; formula read from secondary write-ups); WILDS reports worst-group; Shifts scores error-retention AUC (https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07455); temperature scaling fit on iid data fails under shift while about 5-member ensembles stay best (Ovadia et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530); Group-DRO gains need strong L2/early stopping (https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731). Use: exact metric reimplementation, per-slice OOF table, worst-slice weighting in the loss, calibration fitted on grouped OOF. Converges with metric-and-decoding (worst-group, bottom-quantile terms, per-slot OOF calibration). Tag: GENERAL ML PRINCIPLE.

**E7 (2026-10-02) "Stump the AI" may mean selection against the baseline.** AFLite and HLE filter items that reference models solve; resulting rankings are unstable, disadvantage adversary-like models and oversample low-agreement/noisy items (https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04108; https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08181; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249). Hypothesis for Eris: reproduce the obvious agent recipe on train under random and several grouped CVs; the scheme whose score matches the printed baseline estimates the hidden split's hardness; a match nowhere suggests adversarial selection or a metric/handling mismatch; avoid over-confident predictions on noisy hard rows. Extends V11 (reproduce published reference numbers). Tag: UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS.

**E8 (2026-10-02) Contamination and shortcut magnitudes for sanity checks.** CIFAR test duplicates 3.3%/10% (dedup lowers accuracy 9-14% relative; https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00423); subject-leaky tile splits inflate scores up to 41% (https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06539); test label errors at least 3.3% on average, rankings flip when corrected (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14749); hypothesis-only NLI 67% (https://aclanthology.org/N18-2017.pdf); 294 leakage-affected papers (https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048). Use: fixed train-only audit (duplicates, sibling groups, id/order/metadata-only and partial-input controls, label-noise estimate) and record the CV inflation each causes; never exploit them. Converges with V2, V10, V12; magnitudes and partial-input/shuffled-label controls are new. Tag: PUBLIC RESEARCH.
