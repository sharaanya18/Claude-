## Scope A: generalisation to a hidden leaderboard

**P-A01 (2026-10-02) · PUBLIC RESEARCH · public-slice noise.** Binomial error bars are about +-7% at n=100 and +-2% at n=1000, and a Kaggle public/private pair with n=30/28 differed by about +-15%; in the SIIM-ISIC winner's 18 single models the score std was 0.0012 (cv_all), 0.0043 (cv_2020), 0.0060 (private), 0.0093 (public), and public correlated "essentially 0" with CV for single models. Proposal: express every public score as a z-score against CV using se_pub = std(OOF row loss)/sqrt(n_pub) (bootstrap of n_pub OOF rows for AUC/F1), treat |z|<2 as noise, and never compute CV-LB correlation from <=5 submissions. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.07581 [S]; https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351 [S]. Cross-check: extends CLAUDE.md 4A and platform-facts 6.3 (adds the number).

**P-A06 (2026-10-02) · GENERAL ML PRINCIPLE · derive the split from the generator; measure the random-vs-group gap.** Group by the largest implied unit, time-order with a gap/embargo for temporal data, use StratifiedGroupKFold for rare labels; the random-vs-group CV gap on the same model is the leakage/shift magnitude. **P-A07 (2026-10-02) · PUBLIC RESEARCH · corrected paired test (refines V4).** Use the Nadeau-Bengio corrected resampled t: t = mean(d)/sqrt((1/(r*k) + n_test/n_train)*var(d)), df=r*k-1, with d the paired per-fold differences on identical splits. Arithmetic from the formula: naive SE is too small by about 1.5x at 5-fold, 1.45x at 10-fold, 2.2x for 3 repeats of 5 folds; Varoquaux reports fold-SE underestimates error bars by ~0.7 even in the best case; Bates-Hastie-Tibshirani show naive CV intervals can have coverage far below nominal; Bouckaert-Frank recommend 10x10 CV with this correction. Sources: http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1661-inference-for-the-generalization-error.pdf [S]; https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00673 [S]; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24775-3_3 [S]. Cross-check: V4 says "1 SE of the paired fold differences"; this suggests a stricter bar. Untested locally.

**P-A08 (2026-10-02) · PUBLIC RESEARCH · proportionate selection discipline and winner's curse.** Flat CV is adequate for choosing among a few model families (nested rarely changes the winner: 12 algorithms x 115 datasets) but optimistic as a reported number; overtuning (selected config worse than default) occurred in ~10% of HPO benchmark cases, worst for small data and holdout validation; selection bias grows with the candidate pool (order-statistics view). Arithmetic [R]: expected maximum of N standard normals is 1.16/1.54/1.87/2.25/2.51 for N=5/10/20/50/100, so with selection noise sigma=0.005 the best of 20 equal configs looks ~0.009 better than truth. Proposal: state N, report best_cv minus E[max]*sigma_sel, cross-fit blends/thresholds/decode constants on group halves. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.09446 [S]; https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19540 [S]; https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03742 [S]; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1758-2946-6-10 [S]. Cross-check: converges with V3; adds the arithmetic and the cost split.

**P-A09 (2026-10-02) · GENERAL ML PRINCIPLE · fresh-seed confirmation of finalists.** Own CV folds are a reused test set; after choosing finalists on working folds, re-score the top 2 on 3 fresh split seeds (re-drawn groups) plus the untouched sanity holdout and ship the winner (or the simpler of a tie). Rationale: the Ladder's principle of crediting only improvements beyond noise; model similarity limits but does not remove overuse. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04585 [S]; https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12580 [S]. Cross-check: extends V4 (sanity holdout).
