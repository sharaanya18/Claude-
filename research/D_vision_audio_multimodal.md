## Area 1. Backbones, the capacity ladder and fine-tuning recipes

**D1. Backbone shortlist: CNNs win low-data fine-tuning in benchmarks, foundation ViTs win elsewhere; decide by one paired local run, not by prior.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: a 2024 multi-domain benchmark (natural, medical, galaxy, remote-sensing) with identical fine-tuning settings found ConvNeXt-T, EfficientNetV2-S and RegNet the most consistent backbones and attention models "tend to perform poorly under low data finetuning" (arXiv 2406.05612, [S] abstract + summary). Counter-evidence in the other direction: the playbook's flood-photo winner used frozen DINOv2-L at 448 (vision.md); ISIC-2024's 1st place trained EVA and EdgeNeXt image models then GBDT (search snippet, [R]); a 2026 font-classification study had DINOv2 + LoRA 98.7 % vs full fine-tune 93.4 % vs linear probe 36.6 % at ~575 images/class (arXiv 2602.13889, [S] abstract; note the data are synthetic). Reconcile: CNN full fine-tune is the cheap strong default for photo-like data with domain gap; DINOv2/EVA02/SigLIP2 LP-FT is the default when the signal is fine-grained or the label set is semantic. Gain: the choice of rung/backbone is typically the largest single lever (matches S-tier principle 5). Compute: public timm train throughput on an RTX 3090 (AMP, NHWC, torch 1.12, img/s): resnet50 1218, convnext_tiny 819, convnext_small 487, convnext_base 339, efficientnetv2_rw_s 521, vit_small/16 1058, vit_base/16 393, swin_tiny 649 ([S] timm results CSV). Inference img/s on the same card: convnext_tiny 3120, convnext_base 1310, eva02_base_p14_224 1111, vit_base_siglip_224 1566, dinov2 ViT-B/14 at 518 px 182 ([S] timm results CSV). The A10G has 600 GB/s memory bandwidth and ~70 TFLOPS dense bf16 (spec aggregator, [R]); the 3090 has ~936 GB/s (from memory), so plan 0.6-0.9x of the 3090 numbers and profile (UNTESTED factor). Compliance: all candidates are real fine-tunes or LP-FT; frozen-probe-only is a yardstick (platform-facts section 5). Cross-check: independently confirms the "frozen probe -> LP-FT -> full" ladder in ensembling-and-training.md E-B and the "simplest won" principle; adds the CNN-first evidence and the throughput table. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05612 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13889 ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/main/results/benchmark-train-amp-nhwc-pt112-cu113-rtx3090.csv ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nhwc-pt240-cu124-rtx3090.csv ; https://flopper.io/gpu/nvidia-a10g-24gb

**D2. Weight provenance: torchvision `weights=` and other non-HF hosts break the "HF/timm only" rule.** [VERIFIED PLATFORM REQUIREMENT] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: CLAUDE.md section 1 allows internet only to pull pretrained weights from Hugging Face / timm; platform-facts.md section 4 repeats "HF/timm weights only (never GitHub, never self-hosted)". torchvision's COCO/ImageNet weights are fetched from `https://download.pytorch.org/models/...` (e.g. `fasterrcnn_resnet50_fpn_v2_coco-dd69338a.pth`, box mAP 46.7; `weights_backbone` defaults to None) ([S] torchvision source), which is neither HF nor timm. So `fasterrcnn_resnet50_fpn_v2(weights="DEFAULT")` and `torchvision.models.resnet50(weights=...)` are a latent Prompt-Compliance risk, and any checkpoint loaded from GitHub releases, Kaggle Models, TF Hub or Zenodo is out (this is why BEATs/PANNs/EfficientAT/Perch, widely used in audio write-ups, are not directly usable; hosting locations from memory, [R]). Safe path: timm backbone (weights on the HF hub under `timm/`) + torchvision detection/segmentation *classes* instantiated with random heads (`BackboneWithFPN`), or `transformers` models whose checkpoints live on the HF hub. Also unverified-but-prudent: anything needing `trust_remote_code=True` downloads code, not only weights. Gain: avoids a rejection that would zero the run. Cost: none. Cross-check: new application of platform-facts section 4 to torchvision/audio checkpoints; vision.md only says "banned task-specific checkpoints". Sources: CLAUDE.md section 1; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/vision/main/torchvision/models/detection/faster_rcnn.py

**D4. LP-FT is the default rung between probe and full fine-tune; ERM++ shows its ingredients stack.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: Kumar et al. (ICLR 2022): full fine-tuning gains ~2 % in-distribution but loses ~7 % out-of-distribution versus a linear probe when features are good and the shift is large (feature distortion); LP-FT (train the head on frozen features, then fine-tune everything) gives ~+1 % ID and ~+10 % OOD over plain fine-tuning across 10 shift datasets ([S] arXiv 2202.10054 abstract). ERM++ (Teterwak et al.) assembles train-longer, full-data refit after HP selection, strong initialisation (DINOv2 for ViT-B), a classifier warm start (head-only for the first 500 steps, 2500 for ViT; wording from summariser), model-parameter averaging, unfrozen BN and attention-only tuning; reports +5 % (ResNet-50) and +15 % (ViT-B/16) over prior ERM on five DomainBed datasets ([S] abstract; per-component ablation numbers from the summariser looked garbled, do not quote). Recipe: stage 1 head only with backbone frozen (a few epochs, cheap), stage 2 unfreeze with head LR >> backbone LR (backbone 1e-5..5e-5 for ViT-B, 10x lower than the head), layer-wise decay, EMA. Compute: stage 1 is nearly free; stage 2 is one normal fine-tune. Compliance: real parameter updates; assert backbone parameters moved (G7). Cross-check: independently confirms vision.md/E12 LP-FT and the multimodal-robotics.md LP-FT note; adds the OOD numbers and the ERM++ stack as an ablation order. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01973 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.01973

**D5. Weight-space averaging (WiSE-FT, soups, SWAD, EMA) is the cheapest robustness gain and needs no target data.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: WiSE-FT linearly interpolates the initial (zero-shot or LP) weights with the fine-tuned weights, +4-6 pp under distribution shift on ImageNet variants, +2 to +23 pp on six more shifts, +0.8-3.3 pp in-distribution on seven transfer sets, no extra inference or training cost ([S] arXiv 2109.01903 abstract). Model soups average several fine-tunes from one init; greedy soup (add a model only if held-out accuracy improves) beats the best single model at no inference cost ([S] search summary of arXiv 2203.05482). SWAD averages weights densely every iteration with start/end chosen by validation loss, +1.6 % mean OOD over five DG benchmarks ([S] arXiv 2102.08604 abstract). Practical form for a 1 h budget: keep EMA (decay ~0.999 over a few thousand steps) plus an interpolation coefficient alpha between the LP-stage state and the end state chosen on grouped hold-out OOF; one extra state_dict, no extra epochs. Compliance: deterministic, in-script, no test use; selection of alpha must be nested (V3). Cross-check: independently confirms E-A "snapshot/EMA averaging, greedy soup (D4/D13, G12)"; new: alpha-interpolation to the LP state and SWAD's overfit-aware window. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08604

**D6. A rung between LP-FT and full fine-tune: update fewer or selected parameters (attention-only, surgical, freeze-embedding, hand-rolled LoRA).** [UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS] 2026-10-02.
Evidence converges from independent public sources: fine-tuning only the self-attention weights of a ViT beats tuning the FFN and saves compute (Touvron et al., "Three things", [R] snippet); ERM++ attention-tuning +1.6 % on ViT-B ([S] summariser, treat as direction); surgical fine-tuning of a chosen block subset matches or beats full fine-tuning under shift, early layers for input-level shifts such as corruptions (ICLR 2023, [S] abstract); freezing the <1 % embedding layer lets SGD match AdamW OOD (76.7 vs 76.0 vs 72.0, [S] snippet of Kumar and Shen 2022); LoRA beat full fine-tune on a ~575 images/class DINOv2 task (98.7 vs 93.4, D1). Expected gain: +0.5-2 points on small/shifted data over full fine-tune, nothing guaranteed. Compute: lower than full fine-tune. Compliance: still genuine training; `peft` is not on the verified allowed list, so implement LoRA in ~30 lines or restrict to attention-only unfreezing. Conflict to resolve locally: ensembling-and-training.md E6 says "full fine-tune beats LoRA on small data", which was observed on text; the vision evidence above points the other way. Test on grouped CV with a paired comparison before adopting. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11466 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09359 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09795

**D7. Fine-tuning defaults that every modern recipe shares, with the optimiser plumbing that exists in timm.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Defaults: AdamW, weight decay 0.05, cosine with linear warmup, label smoothing 0.1, drop-path (stochastic depth), grad-clip 1.0, EMA, layer-wise LR decay (ConvNeXt/ViT fine-tune settings in the literature use ~0.7-0.9; verify on CV; snippet [R]), mixup 0.8 / cutmix 1.0 only when data are not tiny. "How to train your ViT" (Steiner et al.): judicious augmentation + regularisation on ImageNet-1k matches 10x larger plain ImageNet-21k pretraining, so on small data keep regularisation on, and use lower LRs than ResNet recipes (SGD momentum 0.9, batch 512, cosine, clip 1.0 in the paper; [R] snippet). timm has `timm.optim.create_optimizer_v2(model, "adamw", lr=..., weight_decay=..., layer_decay=0.75)` with param-group handling that exempts bias/norm from weight decay, and a `param_group_fn` hook for head-vs-backbone LRs ([S] timm v1.0.30 docs); `ModelEmaV3` exists in recent timm (not confirmed on the docs page I could load; a 10-line manual EMA is safe). EfficientNetV2 progressive learning raises image size *and* regularisation strength (dropout, RandAug, mixup) together across stages; resizing alone with fixed regularisation loses accuracy ([S] search summary of arXiv 2104.00298), a free speed-up for CNNs when training resolution is high. Compliance: all fixed schedules, no clock. Cross-check: independently confirms E-C "AdamW, warmup+cosine, layer-wise decay, EMA"; adds the progressive-learning coupling and the exact timm hooks. Sources: https://huggingface.co/docs/timm/reference/optimizers ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10270 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00298

## Area 2. Resolution, TTA and ensembling

**D8. Resolution: train lower, test about 1.15x higher (FixRes), and bump resolution only where details are small.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: with random-resized-crop training and centre-crop testing, objects appear ~0.8x their training size at test time; raising the test crop (about 1.15x) removes the mismatch, and fine-tuning only BN/classifier at the test resolution cheaply compensates statistics (FixRes, arXiv 1906.06423, [S] summary). Touvron et al. report that training at lower resolution and fine-tuning at the target resolution saves compute and often improves target-resolution accuracy ([R] snippet). timm tags already encode this for CNNs (EfficientNetV2-S train 300 / test 384). For ViTs, position embeddings must be interpolated (timm `dynamic_img_size`/`img_size=` arguments; verify on the installed version). Gain: typically +0.3-1 point for free at inference; larger for small-object tasks (the playbook's DINOv2-L@448 result). Compute: inference cost scales ~(res ratio)^2 for CNNs, more for ViTs (attention). Compliance: fixed resolutions chosen on grouped OOF, not on test. Cross-check: complements vision.md "downsizing small details"; adds the train/test-resolution gap and the EfficientNetV2 recipe. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06423 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00298

**D9. TTA: average only on transformations the model saw in training, validate on OOF, and note that averaging can flip correct predictions.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: Shanmugam et al. (ICCV 2021) show that even when TTA improves mean accuracy it converts many correct predictions into incorrect ones, and a learned aggregator beats the plain average ([S] abstract/summary). In practice: use {identity, hflip} first; add multi-scale only if D8 shows resolution sensitivity; average logits (not hard labels); for detection use box fusion (D11) rather than NMS over union. Direction-bearing outputs (angles, left/right keypoints, text orientation) must be un-transformed (vision.md already says so). Gain: +0.2-1 point on classification; more for detection/segmentation. Cost: n_views x inference. Compliance: per-sample TTA is explicitly allowed (CLAUDE.md 2.2) unless the description bans TTA/multi-view; a learned aggregator must be fit on OOF only. Cross-check: independently confirms vision.md TTA allowance; adds the "can hurt" caveat and OOF-gating. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11156

**D10. Deep-net K-fold: fold-model ensemble versus 100 % refit is an open, testable trade-off.** [UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS] 2026-10-02.
validation-recipes.md V9 says refit on 100 % with fixed counts (recorded +0.007 to +0.04 versus fold-averaged ~80 % models); ERM++ also lists "full data after HP selection" as a gain (+1.2 % on ResNet-50 per summariser). For fine-tuned CNN/ViT, however, K fold models give K-way variance reduction for free and reuse their OOF for blend/post-processing, while a refit costs one more full training and loses ensembling unless several seeds are refit. Under a 1 h budget the realistic options are (a) 5 fold models averaged, (b) 5 folds for OOF/plan then 2-3 full-data refits averaged. Test on one local task with the paired-fold rule (V4) before choosing; record which one wins by data size (the V9 evidence was on small site-grouped sets where 20 % of groups matter). Compliance: both fixed-count; refit epoch count = mean best epoch x 1.1 from CV. Cross-check: refines V9/D13 for deep nets; not a contradiction of V9.

## Area 3. Detection, segmentation, keypoints, OCR/documents

**D11. Detection: strong backbone, mosaic + mixup, 5-fold, multi-scale models, then weighted boxes fusion and TTA; pseudo-labelling on test is the part that must be dropped.** [COMPETITION PATTERN] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: Global Wheat Detection 1st place: EfficientDet D5-D7 at 512-1024 px plus Faster R-CNN FPN with ResNet152 at 1024 px, 5-fold, custom mosaic + MixUp + heavy photometric/blur/noise augmentation, multi-scale ensemble with Weighted Boxes Fusion and TTA, then two rounds of pseudo-labelling the test images (private 0.7175 -> 0.7205) ([S] repo README). Transferable: mosaic/mixup/WBF/TTA/multi-resolution. Not transferable here: pseudo-labelling the test set (CLAUDE.md 2.3 #5). WBF averages coordinates of overlapping boxes across models instead of discarding them (Solovyev et al.); the `ensemble-boxes` package is not on the allowed list, so write the ~40-line fusion by hand and tune IoU/score thresholds on OOF (decision hyperparameters on train-only OOF). HF alternatives with weights on the hub: DETR / RT-DETR(v2) (`PekingU/rtdetr_r18vd`), tutorial recipe 20 epochs, LR 5e-5, warmup 300, max grad-norm 0.1, batch 16 ([R] Roboflow tutorial snippet). Gain: WBF + TTA is commonly reported as a +0.02-0.05 mAP-class lift over a single model/NMS (public Global Wheat notebooks, [R], figures not verified). Compute: one detector at 1024 px is ~1-2 min/epoch on a few thousand images (extrapolated, UNTESTED); plan 2-3 members x 5 folds only when images are < ~3k. Compliance: backbone from timm (D2); copy-paste/mosaic of real labelled content is allowed (E-C), synthetic generation is not. Cross-check: vision.md "Detection + selective review" has the decode side; this adds training/ensemble recipe. Sources: https://github.com/dungnb1333/Global-Wheat-Dection-1st-Place ; https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/rt_detr_v2 ; https://blog.roboflow.com/train-rt-detr-custom-dataset-transformers/

**D12. Segmentation and keypoint recipes: timm-encoder U-Net or SegFormer, composite loss, upsample logits, subpixel heatmap decode.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Segmentation: smp-style U-Net with a timm encoder is the Kaggle default (BCE + Dice, Lovasz for IoU metrics, deep supervision, flip TTA; search snippet [R]); `segmentation_models_pytorch` is not on the allowed list, so build the decoder yourself over `timm.create_model(..., features_only=True)`. HF SegFormer (`nvidia/mit-b0..b5`, `nvidia/segformer-b*-finetuned-ade-*`) returns logits at **1/4 resolution** (the docs example shows `[1, 150, 128, 128]` for a 512 input) so upsample before loss and metric, uses `semantic_loss_ignore_index=255`, and its docs recommend random crop/pad to a fixed square (512 or 640) ([S] HF SegFormer docs). Touching instances: playbook J1 already covers geometric channels + watershed. Keypoints: heatmap regression with continuous (sub-pixel) Gaussian targets and local soft-argmax decode reaches sub-pixel accuracy at low heatmap resolution (Bulat et al. BMVC 2021, [S] search summary); with flip-test the left/right keypoint index permutation must be applied to the flipped heatmaps. Gain: decode details are worth +1-3 % on localisation metrics in the cited work (author-reported, [R]). Compute: 256-512 px U-Net with a ConvNeXt-T/EfficientNet encoder trains at several hundred img/s class throughput on an A10G (extrapolated, UNTESTED). Compliance: encoder weights from timm (D2). Cross-check: complements vision.md segmentation line (GroupNorm for small batches, EMA, transform-aware TTA); adds the 1/4-resolution and ignore-index pitfalls and the keypoint decode. Sources: https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/segformer ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02360

**D13. OCR and document tasks: fine-tune an encoder-decoder (TrOCR/Donut) with photometric+geometric augmentation; avoid NC-licensed layout models and bespoke OCR engines as the core.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: simple fine-tuning with augmentation is a "surprisingly effective" domain-adaptation baseline for handwriting recognition and resists overfitting even on very small target sets (arXiv 2302.06308, [S] snippet); a TrOCR medieval-HTR ablation reports every augmentation family (small rotations, blur, brightness/contrast, translation, speckle/stain overlays) improving CER/WER, with a top-5 voting ensemble reaching CER 1.60 versus 1.86 single (arXiv 2606.24302, [R]). TrOCR = BEiT image encoder + RoBERTa decoder, 0.3B params, single text-line input; Donut (MIT) = Swin encoder + BART decoder for whole-page parsing ([S] model cards). From-scratch regimes: CRNN + CTC on the provided lines is the standard small-data answer (general knowledge). Compliance: `easyocr`/`pytesseract` are in the allowed list but running a pretrained OCR engine is inference-only and fails the strip-the-ML test (CLAUDE.md 2.1); `layoutlmv3-base` is CC-BY-NC-SA (D3). Gain: augmentation + ensemble gives ~15 % relative CER in the cited ablation (author-reported). Compute: TrOCR-base fine-tune on a few thousand lines is minutes per epoch on an A10G (extrapolated, UNTESTED). Cross-check: new (vision.md has no OCR entry except diagram structure). Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06308 ; https://huggingface.co/microsoft/trocr-base-printed ; https://huggingface.co/naver-clova-ix/donut-base

## Area 4. Audio and signal

**D14. Audio default: mel-spectrogram into an ImageNet-pretrained timm CNN, 5-s windows, SED/attention pooling, wave-level mixup, SpecAugment at moderate strength, speaker/site-grouped CV.** [COMPETITION PATTERN] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: BirdCLEF 2025 winners and the 2026 write-ups that reuse them used mel frontends (n_fft 1024, hop 320, 128 mels, fmin 20, fmax 16000 at 32 kHz is the quoted recipe, [R] search snippet of the 2026 27th-place write-up that I could not open) into timm CNNs (ConvNeXtV2, HGNet, NFNet, EfficientNet), mixup with p = 0.5 during fine-tuning, BCE loss, and a diverse ensemble (SED-head CNNs, MLP-head CNNs, taxon specialists). A public BirdCLEF 2025 repo found `aug_prob` 0.5 best, constant padding of short clips beat copy-padding, 5-fold local AUC 0.945-0.956 but public LB 0.73-0.78, so the local/public gap was large and mel height (n_mels) interacts with the backbone ([S] repo README). SpecAugment: masking around 10-25 % of time/frequency is a good range, >40 % destroys the signal (SpecAugment++ comparison, [R]); mixing magnitude spectrograms is hard to disentangle, so mix waveforms. Single-channel input: `timm.create_model(..., pretrained=True, in_chans=1)` sums the RGB conv weights ([S] timm issue discussion), or replicate to 3 channels with delta/chroma-like extra channels as a hand-crafted input to the deep model. HMS brain-activity (Kaggle): 10-fold GroupKFold grouped by patient, two-stage training (all labels, then only examples with >= 10 annotator votes at a lower LR), L/R channel-flip augmentation (a physically valid symmetry), KL loss on vote distributions; 1D-EEG model beat the spectrogram model and fusing both added a little ([S] public write-up by R. Peters, ranked 60/2767). Compliance: winners' extra data (older BirdCLEF/xeno-canto years), Perch v2 / AudioProtoPNet distillation, and noisy-student pseudo-labelling of test soundscapes are external data, non-HF weights or test adaptation here; do not copy those steps. Cross-check: independently confirms audio-signal.md "group by recording/speaker" and "structurally different heads"; adds mel/mixup/SpecAugment specifics and the label-quality two-stage trick (also a mirror of site-weighted/quality-aware loss). Sources: https://github.com/detkov/BirdCLEFplus_2025 ; https://www.ryanirl.com/blog/hbac/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16858 ; https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/issues/2445

**D15. Pretrained audio encoders that live on the HF hub, and how to fine-tune them.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
AST (`MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593`, BSD-3, 86.6M; paper 95.6 % ESC-50, a community fine-tune 94.64 %, both [S]/[R]) fine-tunes as a ViT on spectrograms; the feature extractor normalises to mean 0 / std 0.5 and pads/crops to a fixed frame count (default 1024 frames, ~10 s; verify). Wav2Vec2-family in `transformers`: `Wav2Vec2ForSequenceClassification` with `use_weighted_layer_sum=True` (learned softmax over layers, SUPERB style), `mask_time_prob=0.05` default (a SpecAugment-style masking applied to latent frames), `layerdrop=0.1` default; for `feat_extract_norm == "group"` models (wav2vec2-base) do **not** pass `attention_mask`, pad with zeros; for "layer"-norm models (lv60 style) do ([S] HF Wav2Vec2 docs). `WhisperForAudioClassification` exists with `use_weighted_layer_sum` and `classifier_proj_size=256` ([S] HF Whisper docs); check Whisper's fixed-length input requirement before feeding short clips. SUPERB-style evidence: weighted sums over frozen layers are a strong cheap baseline, and emotion/content tasks lean on the top layers ([R] search summary), a good yardstick for the LP rung. Whisper ASR-as-zero-shot-classifier (arXiv 2311.09363) is inference-only and fails the strip-the-ML test. Synthetic audio augmentation (Synthio, ICLR 2025) generates new labelled audio with diffusion models and is banned (CLAUDE.md 2.3 #6). Compute: AST-B on 10 s clips is ViT-B-class (see D1 table) with ~1212 tokens per clip (AST paper, from memory), i.e. much more attention cost than 224 px images; wav2vec2-base on 5 s clips is cheap. Compliance: HF weights only (D2); speaker-disjoint folds when the test is speaker-disjoint (audio-signal.md). Cross-check: independently confirms audio-signal.md "frozen speech encoders as feature sources"; adds the fine-tune switches and the hosting/licence facts. Sources: https://huggingface.co/MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593 ; https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/wav2vec2 ; https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/whisper ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02056

## Area 5. Multimodal and small VLM fine-tuning

**D16. Multimodal fusion and small-VLM fine-tuning: end-to-end image+metadata heads with modality dropout, deep-OOF-into-GBDT as a minor member, and size-matched VLM adaptation.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Fusion: ISIC-2024 top solutions trained image models (EVA, EdgeNeXt, EfficientNet) and fed their out-of-fold predictions as meta-features to GBDTs alongside patient/lesion tabular data; the 2nd place used 0-3 image models' predictions as meta-features with HPO per configuration ([R] search summary of several repos/posts). On this platform the deep model must stay the primary and the GBDT is a minor, cross-fitted member (CLAUDE.md 2.5 "ensembling a fine-tuned model with a non-DL model: grey"). The compliant alternative is an end-to-end head: image embedding concatenated (or FiLM-modulated) with tabular/hand-crafted features inside the deep model, trained with modality dropout (randomly zeroing a whole modality embedding) which improves robustness to missing/noisy modalities ([R] summary of MICCAI 2025 improved-modality-dropout and related). Early versus late fusion is context-dependent (late fusion degrades less with small training sets, [R]), so compare both on grouped CV. Small VLMs: SmolVLM (Apache-2.0) full fine-tune, LoRA or QLoRA, ~16 GB on an L4 with 8-bit + gradient checkpointing, DocVQA 81.6 test ([S] HF blog); Qwen2-VL-2B (Apache-2.0, DocVQA 90.1, OCRBench 794, dynamic resolution via `min_pixels`/`max_pixels`, needs recent transformers) ([S] model card); a 500M-class model can be fully fine-tuned in 24 GB, a 2B model needs LoRA or an 8-bit optimiser (arithmetic, not measured). Avoid gated PaliGemma (D3). `peft` and `bitsandbytes` are not on the verified allowed list (D3), so hand-roll LoRA or choose a model small enough for full fine-tune. Compute: VLM epochs on a few thousand examples are tens of minutes (extrapolated, UNTESTED); budget a single run. Cross-check: multimodal-robotics.md covers episode-level fusion; this adds the stacking/dropout/VLM size facts. Sources: https://huggingface.co/blog/smolvlm ; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18284

**D17. Initialise the classification head from CLIP/SigLIP text embeddings and fine-tune contrastively or with WiSE-FT, when class names are semantic.** [UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: FLYP fine-tunes both towers with the original contrastive loss, casting labels as text prompts, and outperforms standard fine-tuning and linear-head methods across 7 distribution-shift, 6 transfer and 3 few-shot benchmarks (arXiv 2212.00638, [S] snippet); WiSE-FT (D5) on top of a zero-shot-initialised head preserves the zero-shot robustness. Where labels are meaningful words (species, scene types, defects) a text-derived head is a strong LP-FT starting point with no extra data; with anonymised classes it does not apply. Compute: CLIP ViT-B/16 fine-tune is ViT-B class (D1 table); `transformers.CLIPModel` provides both towers from the HF hub (timm `*clip*` tags hold the vision tower only). Compliance: training of a general-purpose pretrained model, allowed (CLAUDE.md 2.2); classnames used must come from train metadata, not test. Expected gain: unknown for Eris; the published gains are largest in few-shot and shifted settings. Test as one arm of the baseline experiment. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00638

## Area 6. Small-data tricks that hold on a private split

**D18. Augmentation and regularisation do the work of extra data: TrivialAugment as the tuning-free default, plus real-sample mosaic/copy-paste, never generated samples.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
TrivialAugment (Müller and Hutter, ICCV 2021) applies one uniformly sampled augmentation at a uniformly sampled strength per image, has no search/tuning, and matches or beats RandAugment/AutoAugment on CIFAR, SVHN and ImageNet ([S] abstract/summary); it is `torchvision.transforms.TrivialAugmentWide` (use on uint8 tensors; fixed seeds). AugReg shows augmentation + regularisation on ImageNet-1k reaches the accuracy of 10x larger plain pretraining for ViTs (D7). "Pushing the limits of simple pipelines for few-shot learning" (CVPR 2022): a plain pretrain -> task fine-tune pipeline with strong augmentation, DINO/CLIP-style ViTs, beats meta-learning; source data and architecture dominate (CVPR 2022, [S] snippet). Real-component copy-paste, mosaic and mixup are real-data augmentation (E-C, J5); diffusion/GAN-generated labelled data (e.g. Synthio for audio, synthetic fonts/text-in-image) counts as synthetic data and is banned (CLAUDE.md 2.3 #6). Keep augmentations physics-valid (vision.md: handedness, text orientation, L/R symmetry in EEG as used in D14). Mixup/cutmix can hurt tiny datasets and fine-grained or counting tasks: gate on grouped CV. Compute: ~zero when augmentation runs on GPU batches (D23). Cross-check: independently confirms vision.md "strong augmentation is load-bearing on tens of samples" and E-C "augment only with label-preserving transforms"; adds the tuning-free default. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10158 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07305

**D19. Group-aware CV for images and audio: derive groups by embedding near-duplicate clustering, split by patient/site/speaker, balance rare classes, and train quality-aware.** [COMPETITION PATTERN] 2026-10-02.
Mechanism: SIIM-ISIC melanoma top solutions used a patient-isolated, positive-balanced "triple stratified" K-fold, and found near-duplicate images by pushing all images through a pretrained CNN, kNN on the embeddings and manual verification, because duplicates across train/validation inflate local scores; they also ensembled models trained at different resolutions (128-1024) for diversity ([S] write-up summary of leocon.dev). HMS (D14) used GroupKFold by patient and treated high-vote and low-vote labels as a bimodal quality structure, which "improved alignment between local CV and leaderboard" ([S]). Recipe for Eris: frozen-embedding cosine kNN (including flipped copies) on train only + union-find into groups, cap giant groups, balance rare labels greedily (V6), report per-group slices; a >0.01 gap between grouped and random CV is the leakage tell. Cost: one frozen forward pass over train. Compliance: grouping uses train only; no test rows touched. Cross-check: independently confirms validation-recipes.md V2 (near-duplicate unions) and V6 (rare-label fold balance), `make_groups.py`; adds the image-embedding recipe, the multi-resolution diversity member, and quality-aware two-stage training. Sources: https://www.leocon.dev/blog/2021/09/kaggle-s-winning-solutions-for-melanoma-detection-part1/ ; https://www.ryanirl.com/blog/hbac/

## Area 7. Robustness when the held-out population differs in kind (no fitting on the target set)

**D20. Domain-generalisation baselines: a carefully tuned ERM with strong initialisation and averaging is the benchmark to beat; specialised DG algorithms rarely add more than a point.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
DomainBed (Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz): when carefully implemented and tuned, ERM matches or beats 14 DG algorithms; no algorithm beats it by more than one point under the same conditions; model selection is part of the algorithm ([S] summary). ERM++ (D4) is the modern realisation (+5 % R-50, +15 % ViT-B/16 over prior ERM on 5 datasets). "Accuracy on the line" (Miller et al., ICML 2021): OOD accuracy is a strong linear function of ID accuracy across architectures, hyperparameters, training-set size and training duration, so pick by grouped-CV score *unless* the shift breaks the line (spurious-correlation datasets) ([S] summary). Group-label-aware options that need only train data: Deep Feature Reweighting (retrain the last layer on a small group-balanced split of *train*, ICLR 2023, [S] summary) for background/site shortcuts. Augmentation families to rehearse the shift: colour/blur/JPEG/resolution jitter for new sensors (J7), random-resized-crop scale range for scale shift, TrivialAugment as the generic one (D18). Multi-backbone diversity is a shift hedge (D21). Compliance: none of this fits anything on test rows. Cross-check: independently confirms validation-recipes.md V7 "rehearse the shift", `learned-patterns.md` L002/L003 (simulated-shift proxies track, row-local models cannot learn a changed mapping), and ensembling "diversity of assumption". New: the ERM++ stack, the accuracy-on-the-line selection rule, DFR. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01434 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01973 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04649 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02937

**D21. Calibration under shift: do not trust an in-distribution temperature; calibrate on leave-group-out OOF, keep shrinkage, and rely on ensembles and larger pretrained models.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
Ovadia et al. (2019): accuracy and calibration degrade as shift grows; temperature scaling is well calibrated on i.i.d. test data but not under dataset shift, while deep ensembles are consistently among the best, especially under larger shift ([S] summary). Minderer et al. (NeurIPS 2021): modern ViT and MLP-Mixer are well calibrated both ID and OOD; under shift calibration improves with model size; architecture matters more than size or pretraining amount ([S] summary). Practical rule for Eris: fit one temperature (and an optional uniform-mix weight) on leave-group-out/leave-cluster-out OOF, cross-fitted (V3), report ECE/Brier per slice, prefer a shrunk temperature for metrics that punish confident wrong rows (multimodal-robotics.md bottom-k), and treat an ensemble of 2-3 structurally different backbones as the shift hedge. Compliance: all calibration constants come from train-only OOF (CLAUDE.md 4A "post-processing tuned on OOF only"). Cross-check: independently confirms metric-and-decoding/V3 cross-fitted calibration; adds the "ID temperature is optimistic under shift" bias statement (V5 style). Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998

**D22. Methods that look like DG baselines but are banned here: test-batch BN statistics, TENT-style adaptation, test-time prototypes/priors, pseudo-labelling.** [VERIFIED PLATFORM REQUIREMENT] 2026-10-02.
CLAUDE.md 2.3 #5 bans using the test set beyond one-sample inference: pseudo-labelling, test-time adaptation, calibrating on the test distribution, fitting scalers/BN statistics on test or train+test; section 2.4 "per-row independence" extends this to any cross-row pooling. Therefore the common DG/TTA tools that update normalisation statistics or class priors from the incoming batch (BN adaptation, entropy minimisation) are out, as are test-pseudo-label rounds that dominate several Kaggle write-ups (BirdCLEF noisy-student on unlabelled soundscapes, Global Wheat two-round pseudo-labelling). `model.eval()` with frozen train BN statistics is the compliant inference mode; per-sample flips/scales are fine. Check that GroupNorm/LayerNorm backbones are preferred if batch-level statistics ever leak at eval. Cross-check: restates engineering-and-compliance.md R "whole-test calibration" for the vision/audio family; the pseudo-label rule is in E-C. Sources: CLAUDE.md 2.3-2.4; `.claude/skills/eris-playbook/references/platform-facts.md` section 5.

## Area 8. Runtime budgeting and determinism for vision/audio on one A10G

**D23. Runtime plan: profile 50 iterations, decode once into RAM, augment on GPU, fix epochs, and size K x epochs x resolution to ~35-40 min.** [GENERAL ML PRINCIPLE] 2026-10-02.
Budget formula: `time ~ folds x epochs x N_train_fold / train_img_per_s + (N_oof + N_test x views) / infer_img_per_s + load/startup`. Use the public timm 3090 numbers in D1 x 0.6-0.9 for the A10G (UNTESTED factor; A10G 600 GB/s, ~70 TFLOPS bf16 dense, 24 GB). Example (derived, not measured): ConvNeXt-T at 224 px ~500 img/s on an A10G -> 5 folds x 8 epochs x 6.4k images ~ 9 min; at 384 px (~3x cost) ~ 26 min; ViT-B/16 at 224 ~250 img/s, DINOv2-B at 518 px inference ~120 img/s. Engineering: bf16 autocast + `channels_last` (on Ampere bf16 and fp16 tensor-core throughput are similar, [R] PyTorch AMP notes; channels_last is the next most important factor for conv-heavy nets after mixed precision, [R]); decode and resize JPEGs once per run into a uint8 tensor at the training resolution plus margin (within-run cache only, E-D), apply flip/crop/colour/TrivialAugment on GPU batches (kornia is on the allowed list; or torchvision v2 ops on tensors) so a fixed small `num_workers` does not bottleneck; free memory between folds; hold a 24 GB headroom check at the largest resolution/batch (OOM is a failed run). Avoid `torch.compile` unless profiled to pay off (compile time and a "backend logic" scrutiny risk under the Deterministic Execution check, UNTESTED). Never use elapsed time to cut epochs, folds or resolution (CLAUDE.md section 3). Cross-check: independently confirms engineering-and-compliance.md section 1 profiling and vision.md "mixed precision + channels_last; fixed workers"; adds the budget formula, the throughput table and the decode-once/GPU-aug pattern. Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/main/results/benchmark-train-amp-nhwc-pt112-cu113-rtx3090.csv ; https://pytorch.org/blog/what-every-user-should-know-about-mixed-precision-training-in-pytorch/ ; https://flopper.io/gpu/nvidia-a10g-24gb

**D24. Vision-specific determinism pitfalls: some CUDA backward ops have no deterministic kernel; use `warn_only=True` and read the first-epoch warnings.** [PUBLIC RESEARCH] 2026-10-02.
`adaptive_avg_pool2d_backward_cuda` has no deterministic implementation and raises under `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` (PyTorch issues #108341/#149130, [S] snippet); bilinear-interpolation backward (used by upsampling decoders, SegFormer-style logits resizing, FPN) was historically non-deterministic and a deterministic CUDA path for non-antialiased 2D bilinear backward was merged recently (PR #197714, [R]). With `warn_only=True` (already the playbook default) the run does not crash, but the output may differ slightly between runs, so verify with the two-run diff in `.claude/scripts/determinism_check.py` and, if drift matters, swap the op (e.g. global pool by `x.mean((2, 3))`, nearest or fixed-weight upsampling, transposed-conv decoder). Do not branch on the device or library version to pick the op (CLAUDE.md section 3 rule 3). Cross-check: independently confirms the GPU determinism checklist in engineering-and-compliance.md section 1; adds the concrete vision ops. Sources: https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/issues/108341 ; https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/randomness.html

## Suggested baseline experiment ladder for any new image task (synthesis, not a decision)

1. Frozen-probe yardstick on a strong feature extractor (DINOv2-B/L or EVA02) with a trained linear head on grouped folds (D1, D4).
2. Arm A: ConvNeXt-T/S (IN-22k/12k tags) full fine-tune at 224-384, TrivialAugment, EMA, label smoothing 0.1. Arm B: LP-FT of DINOv2/EVA02 with layer decay. Optional arm C: attention-only/LoRA (D6), arm D: CLIP head init (D17). Paired grouped-fold comparison (V4); keep the best two structurally different arms.
3. FixRes-style test resolution and flip TTA on OOF (D8, D9); weight-space averaging/alpha (D5).
4. Refit vs fold-ensemble decision (D10); calibrate on leave-group-out OOF (D21); profile and freeze the plan (D23, D24).
