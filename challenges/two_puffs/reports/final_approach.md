# Phase 23 — Final approach

## 1. Problem understanding

Predict, for each participant, nine calibrated probabilities about a **hidden second spirometry
session**, from the participant's **pre-inhaler session only**. The nine events are five
thresholds on the best-FEV1 gain (0/5/10/15/20 %), three on the best-FVC gain (0/5/10 %), and the
ATS best-test criterion (one measure clearing both +200 mL and +12 % by itself).

The crucial fact is that the answers are **not labels** — they are the fraction of 4,000 bootstrap
draws in which the event fired, where a draw takes three acceptable blows with replacement from
each session, takes the best FEV1 and best FVC from each side, and applies the rule. So the target
is a probability generated by a **fully disclosed, runnable construction**, half of whose inputs
(the pre-session) are shipped to us.

Score = `sqrt(RCS × FDS)`: a participant-weighted Brier skill over all nine events, times a
concordance between the implied fragility `4p(1−p)` of our `ats` and the true `4t(1−t)`. The
geometric mean means neither half can be neglected — a constant `ats` caps the score at
`sqrt(RCS × 0.5)`.

## 2. Platform analysis

See `platform_analysis.md`, and the audit of the supplied Solver Guidebook recorded in
`.claude/skills/eris-playbook/references/platform-facts.md`. The operative constraints: one
self-contained `python3 solution.py <public_dir> <submission_out>`; Kaggle-image libraries only;
**no pretrained weights** (this challenge's own rule, and Guidebook §6.5); fixed work plan with no
clock- or machine-dependent code, no try/except fallbacks and no unused method-switching branches
(§3.3); no use of the test set beyond one-participant-at-a-time inference (§5.2 #5); review after

worth score.

## 3. Dataset analysis

See `dataset_audit.md`. Headlines: 1,300 train and 262 test participants, 7,547 graded pre-inhaler
blows, 30,001 further pool blows (37,548 traces decoded in ~15 s + ~57 s). Train and test match on
every demographic margin, so plain stratified participant-disjoint folds are the right mirror of
the hidden split. The decode was verified against the published byte statistics (min byte 18;
37,544/37,548 traces containing a byte below 62). The graded population is obstructed
(FEV1/FVC 0.681); the pool is not (0.812), which is what makes the pool a *normative reference*
rather than extra training data.

## 4. The benchmark discoveries that shaped the solution

1. **The latent is a scalar per arm.** The description's own oracle hands the model "the true
   response magnitude" and nothing else and reaches 0.9116; a model additionally handed the hidden
   session's spread and blow count reaches 0.6003, no better. So the missing information is
   response *size*, and the hidden session's own scatter is worthless.
2. **The pre-side bootstrap is exactly enumerable.** With at most 10 acceptable blows there are at
   most C(12,3) = 220 distinct triples, so the pre-side joint distribution of (best FEV1, best FVC)
   is a closed-form weighted list. The within-session scatter the description points at is not a
   feature to learn — it is an exact part of the forward model.
3. **Inverting the published targets reproduces the published oracle.** Fitting one scalar per arm
   to the eight exceedance probabilities and pushing it back through the bootstrap scores **0.9133
   against the documented 0.9116**. That single number validates the trace decoding, the
   back-extrapolation convention, the FEV1/FVC definitions, the triple rule and the metric
   implementation simultaneously. It is the cheapest and strongest check in the whole project.
4. **Reproducing the published reference rung calibrates the CV harness**: nine-target gradient
   boosting on recomputed indices scores **0.5974 OOF** against the documented 0.6017 held-out.
5. **FDS depends only on the ordering of `|p − 0.5|`.** Any symmetric monotone transform of `ats`
   leaves it unchanged, so the only way to move FDS is a genuinely better fragility signal.
6. **The value-of-information curve says 0.75 is out of reach.** Injecting a noisy copy of the true
   latent and refitting the real pipeline gives: latent R² 0.18 → 0.613, 0.44 → 0.668,
   0.52 → 0.724, 0.58 → 0.782. Reaching 0.75 needs latent R² ≈ 0.55–0.60; the honest achievable
   value from the pre-session is **0.18** (FEV1 arm) and **0.11** (FVC arm). Full reasoning in §15.

## 5. Exact metric

Re-implemented in `official_score` / `somers_d`. Hand-checked against the documented extremes:
perfect → 1.0; 0.5 everywhere → 0.0; replacing `ats` by `1 − ats` leaves FDS at exactly 1.0 (the
documented fragility symmetry); a constant `ats` gives FDS exactly 0.5. Somers' D is normalised by
pairs untied **in the truth**, matching `scipy.stats.somersd(true_fragility, predicted_fragility)`.
Four independent agreements with the description confirm the parsing and the metric at once:
`corr(fev1_10, fvc_05)` 0.4359 vs 0.436; `ats > 0.5` share 22.69 % vs 22.7 %; fragility bands
40.69 / 50.15 / 9.15 % vs 40.7 / 50.2 / 9.2 %; constant-train-mean 0.4828 in-sample vs 0.4705
held-out.

## 6. Validation strategy

One row per participant, so every split is participant-disjoint by construction and no session can
be divided. Folds are **stratified by response band × acceptable-blow count**, exactly as the
challenge's validation tip recommends. 5 folds, repeated over 3 split seeds for the finalists.
Every post-hoc constant — the copula correlation, the blend weight, the isotonic fragility map —
is fitted on out-of-fold predictions and, for the reported number, **cross-fitted** so no fold's
own rows contributed to the map applied to them. Bias direction: the reported OOF number is mildly
**pessimistic** relative to the held-out board, because the fold models see 80 % of the training
participants while the shipped model is refit on 100 % (the reference rung reproduced 0.5974 OOF
against 0.6017 held-out, a gap of +0.004 in the pessimistic direction).

## 7. Feature engineering

205 participant features, all computed from the raw traces plus the shipped demographics:

* **Per blow**: FEV0.5/1/3/6, FVC, PEF, FEF25/50/75, FEF25-75 and each as a fraction of PEF,
  FEV1/FVC, FEV1/FEV6, FEV0.5/FEV1, time to peak flow, volume at peak flow and its fraction of
  FVC, forced expiratory time, back-extrapolated volume and its fraction, a normalised
  descending-limb area and mid-limb flow (concavity), negative-flow sample count, minimum flow,
  flow-interruption count, end-of-test flow, plateau flow, the censoring flag.
* **Session level**: max / min / median / mean / sd / CV / range / relative range / IQR / MAD /
  relative MAD of each index, plus **best-minus-second-best**, **best-minus-median** and the ATS
  150 mL repeatability flags — the within-session disagreement the description says pays.
* **All-blow vs acceptable-only contrasts** (an effort-quality signal), artefact counts, and the
  **blow-order trend** of FEV1/FVC/PEF with the position of the session maximum (a warm-up signal).
* **Pool-fitted normative reference equations**: log-index regressed on log-height, age splines,
  sex and ethnicity over the 6,045 unlabelled pool participants, giving predicted-normal values
  and **percent-predicted ratios**. `pp_fev1_max` is the single strongest feature for the FEV1-arm
  latent, in the clinically expected direction: the more obstructed relative to an unselected
  person of the same build, the larger the response.

## 8. Response modelling (the core)

The hidden session is modelled as the pre-session's blows moved by one latent scalar per arm,
`post_i = pre_i + delta * pre_i**LAM`. Because that transform is increasing in `pre_i`, the best of
a triple maps as `post_best = Fa + delta * Fa**LAM`, and **every one of the nine events collapses to
a single threshold on `delta`**:

```
FEV1 gain >= c   <=>  delta >= ((1+c)*Fb - Fa) / Fa**LAM
ATS on an arm    <=>  delta >= max(0.2 + Fb - Fa, 1.12*Fb - Fa) / Fa**LAM
```

So the whole nine-output problem reduces to learning the **conditional distribution of two
scalars** — vastly more data-efficient than nine noisy regressions on 1,300 rows.

`LAM` was chosen by cross-validation: 1.0 → 0.6231, 0.5 → 0.6217, 0.0 → 0.6218. `LAM = 1.0`
(proportional response) wins and is kept. This was a real test, not an assumption: the inverted
*absolute* change is nearly baseline-independent (r = +0.05 with log FEV1) while the inverted ratio
is not (r = −0.21), which predicted that the absolute parametrisation would be cleaner. It was not.
Recorded as a negative result.

`S(u | x) = P(delta >= u | x)` is learned with an **ordinal ladder**: the threshold level is an
input feature with a monotone-decreasing constraint and every (participant, level) pair is a
training row. That pools strength across thresholds, guarantees a non-increasing survival curve,
is heteroscedastic and shape-free, and **handles the interval-censored participants for free** — a
latent known only to lie below some bound still has a correct 0/1 label at every ladder level
(80 participants are left-censored on the FVC arm, 9 on the FEV1 arm). Three deliberately different
LightGBM configurations are averaged on the logit scale.

The two arms are coupled by a **one-parameter Gaussian copula** (exact Drezner-Wesolowsky
quadrature, verified to 1e-16 against `scipy`), whose correlation is estimated from out-of-fold PIT
values (0.525, against the 0.436 target-level correlation the description publishes).

## 9. Calibration

Calibration is **structural, not post-hoc**. The output is
`p(event) = E_delta[ P_bootstrap(event | delta) ]` under the learned conditional distribution, which
is a calibrated probability whenever that distribution is calibrated, and is non-increasing within
an arm by construction (262/262 test participants monotone in both arms).

That is checked directly rather than assumed: the PIT values of the latent are close to uniform
(deciles 0.120 0.091 0.106 0.088 0.087 0.085 0.086 0.098 0.115 0.124 for the FEV1 arm) and a sweep
of a global shift/spread recalibration of the latent law found **spread = 1.00 optimal**, i.e. there
was nothing left to recalibrate. Nine separate isotonic patches cannot recover this, because they
cannot make the nine outputs mutually coherent.

An earlier version used a single global homoscedastic residual law instead of the conditional
ladder. It scored **0.527** — *worse than the direct nine-target baseline* — because the empirical
residual law is extremely heavy-tailed (kurtosis 30) and far too wide, which pushed every
high-threshold probability up (`fvc_10` mean 0.223 against a truth of 0.083). That failure is what
motivated the conditional-distribution design, and it is the single most instructive experiment in
the log.

## 10. ATS fragility strategy

The Brier-optimal `ats` is `p = E[t|x]`, whose implied fragility is `4p(1−p)`. But the quantity the
grader compares against is `E[4t(1−t)|x] = 4p(1−p) − 4Var(t|x)`. Where the latent posterior is wide
the two orderings disagree badly: a participant who is *certainly* positive-or-negative but about
whom we cannot tell which gets `p ≈ 0.5`, hence maximal *implied* fragility and minimal *true*
fragility.

Three things follow, all measured:

1. **A learned fragility model is worse than the physics.** A LightGBM regressor on the true
   fragility gets Somers' D 0.292; the generative posterior statistic gets 0.360–0.379.
2. **The right statistic is the Borda / expected marginal rank**, not the posterior mean. For a
   pairwise concordance the ideal order puts *i* above *j* when `P(frag_i > frag_j)` is larger;
   averaging over *j* gives `b_i = E_{frag~posterior_i}[F(frag)]` with `F` the population marginal
   CDF of fragility. It differs from `E[frag]` exactly when posterior *shapes* differ, and it
   orders the truth better: D **0.379** vs 0.360 for `E[frag]` vs 0.294 for the implied `4p(1−p)`.
   `F` is built on the **training** participants and reused for test, so nothing is aggregated
   across test rows.
3. **Transfer the ordering, do not blend the values.** Naively blending the fragility-implied
   probability into `ats` buys FDS but pays heavily in RCS (+0.004 net). Instead, weighted
   **isotonic regression** of `|p − 0.5|` on the statistic gives `|p − 0.5|` the statistic's
   ordering at the minimum possible weighted squared cost, with an epsilon tie-break so isotonic's
   flat regions do not create ties that the concordance denominator would punish. That converts
   almost the whole FDS gain into score: FDS 0.647 → 0.689 for an RCS cost of 0.0002.

## 11. Pool usage

The pool is used for exactly one thing: **unsupervised normative reference equations** (§7). It is
never used as labelled data — it is a differently selected, less obstructed population
(FEV1/FVC 0.812 vs 0.681), which the description warns about explicitly. Self-supervised trace
representations and a pool-fitted PCA of loop shape were built and kept as an ablatable block
rather than assumed (see §14).

## 12. Model architecture and ensemble

| member | role | assumption |
|---|---|---|
| ordinal ladder × 3 LightGBM configs, FEV1 arm | conditional survival of `delta_fev1` | latent + exact bootstrap |
| ordinal ladder × 3 LightGBM configs, FVC arm | conditional survival of `delta_fvc` | latent + exact bootstrap |
| Gaussian copula, 1 parameter | couples the two arms for the ATS union | — |
| direct nine-target LightGBM × 9 | minor blend member, and the published reference rung as an in-run yardstick | no latent, no bootstrap |
| weighted isotonic map | transfers the Borda fragility ordering onto `ats` | — |

Blend weight on the direct member is fitted on out-of-fold predictions over a bounded grid
(0 to 0.40 in steps of 0.05) and lands at 0.10–0.20. Everything is refit on 100 % of the training
participants with the same fixed counts before predicting test.

## 13. Overfitting controls

* 5 folds × 3 split seeds; per-fold scores and worst fold reported, not just the mean.
* Every selection step is cross-fitted, and the post-hoc steps are few and low-dimensional: one
  copula correlation, one blend weight on a 9-point bounded grid, one monotone isotonic map.
* The blend grid is capped at 0.40 so the direct member cannot take over on fold noise.
* Candidate count is tracked for winner's-curse bookkeeping (§15).
* Changes were kept only when they held across ladder designs, seeds and the per-fold signs; the
  ladder-design and `LAM` comparisons were both resolved as "within noise, keep the simplest".
* Deliberately *not* done: no feature zoo expansion beyond one ablation round, no deep HPO, no
  stacking on top of the decode until it was measured.

## 15. Why 0.75 is not reachable, stated honestly

The brief sets 0.75+ as the target and asks for evidence rather than assumption. The evidence says
it is not reachable from the pre-inhaler session by any legitimate method, and the reason is
measurable rather than a matter of effort:

* The task reduces **exactly** to predicting one scalar per arm (§4.1), and the reduction is not my
  assumption — it is the construction the organisers used for their own published ceiling, which I
  reproduced to within 0.002.
* The achievable out-of-fold R² of that scalar from every trace-derived index, every within-session
  scatter statistic, every demographic and the pool-derived percent-predicted values is **0.18**
  (FEV1 arm) and **0.11** (FVC arm).
* Feeding the pipeline an artificially improved latent shows what each R² level is worth:
  0.18 → 0.613, 0.44 → 0.668, 0.52 → 0.724, 0.58 → 0.782. **0.75 requires latent R² ≈ 0.55–0.60.**
* The description independently corroborates that this is an information limit, not a modelling
  limit: "What is missing is response size, not observation noise, since a model also handed the
  hidden session's spread and blow count scores 0.6003, no better."

Reaching 0.75 would therefore require roughly tripling the explainable variance of bronchodilator
response from a single pre-inhaler session. Nothing in the shipped data supports that, and the only
routes that would produce such a number are the ones the challenge names as cheating (recovering
the original survey records) or the ones the guidebook bans (fitting on the test set).

So the honest target band is: beat the published best reference (0.6017) with margin, and contest
the printed AI baseline. The achieved out-of-fold number and its fold spread are in §16.

## 16. Final validation

Out-of-fold, final pipeline: **score 0.6240**, RCS 0.5672, FDS 0.6865, mean Brier 0.07870.
Per-fold **0.6251 ± 0.0097**, worst fold **0.6110**. Three split seeds (42/7/2024):
0.6240 / 0.6241 / 0.6267 → **0.6249 ± 0.0015**. Untouched 20 % sanity holdout **0.6246** against
0.6237 on the rest, i.e. no detectable selection optimism. Somers' D of the submitted fragility
0.3788 against 0.2944 for the Brier-optimal probability's implied fragility. Strip-the-ML control
(population-marginal latent, run in-script): 0.5369. Runtime 296 s; two runs byte-for-byte
identical. Full table in `final_report.md`.

## 17. Remaining risks

1. **The modelling assumption.** Everything rests on the hidden session's blows looking like the
   pre-session's rescaled. That is the organisers' own assumption behind the published ceiling, and
   the description says to "treat it as a guide". If the real post-session scatter differs
   systematically, the decode is mildly misspecified — but the misspecification is absorbed into the
   fitted latent because the same assumption is used on both sides of the inversion.
2. **Inverted targets.** The latent targets come from the published train probabilities, so a
   systematic error in my index conventions would propagate. Mitigated by the oracle check.
3. **The isotonic fragility map** is the most flexible post-hoc step. It is cross-fitted for the
   reported number and monotone with one effective shape, but it is the component most likely to
   look better in CV than on the private slice.
4. **262 graded participants** is a small test set. The private slice is a subset of it, so metric
   noise alone is worth roughly ±0.02–0.03 on the final number; FDS is a concordance over pairs and
   is the noisier half.
5. **Compliance.** The decode implements the challenge's published answer construction. I judge
   that sanctioned ("it costs nothing to match them") and not a hand rule, since it is a forward
   model over a *learned* latent distribution and the strip-the-ML control is reported in-script.
   A reviewer applying §5.3 very strictly could still see it differently; that view is recorded
   here rather than hidden.
