# U10 — Prior-art recommendation with contentless candidates (user-supplied, verbatim)

## Overview
Given the title and abstract of a scholarly work, rank the works it cites. The candidates are contentless. Each is an opaque identifier: no title, no abstract, no year, no field, no subject. What a candidate is can only be inferred from the company it keeps - the training works that cite it, and the other candidates it is cited alongside. Each test query is one work. Submit an ordered list of 50 candidates from a pool of 29,679. The references of a query are typically a handful of that pool.

## What makes this hard
A candidate cannot be matched to a query by comparing texts, because a candidate has no text. The only way to place a candidate in the space of ideas is through the works that cited it in the training period: their abstracts, their fields, and which other candidates they cited in the same breath. Popularity is a weak guide and a trap: a short list of candidates is cited by a large minority of works, so always answering with the most-cited earns something but little, and the same little on every query. Real credit comes from candidates that only the right query would cite. The split is temporal: training works published before 2023, test works from 2023 onward. Citation habits drift, new candidates accumulate citations at different rates, and nothing about a test work's own period is available in training.

## Evaluation
For each query the ranking is scored by NDCG@50: a reference found at rank r contributes 1/log2(r+1), divided by what a perfect ranking of that query's references would score. Final score = mean over test queries, 0..1, higher better. Each query is scored on its own.
Reference points on the sealed test set: 50 random candidates 0.0007; the most-cited candidates (same list for every query) 0.0236; tf-idf centroid of training abstracts citing each candidate ranked by cosine similarity to the query text 0.2022; the same nudged by candidate popularity 0.2107. The strongest reference solution reaches 0.2107; its per-query median is 0.158 and it scores exactly zero on 22.8% of queries.

## Dataset (./dataset/public/)
train_queries.parquet: 25,618 works published before 2023 (query_id, title, abstract, year, field). train_references.csv: which pool candidates each training work cites (query_id, candidate_id). test_queries.parquet: 10,106 works published from 2023 onward, to rank (same columns). candidates.parquet: the 29,679 candidates (candidate_id, train_citations = number of training queries citing that candidate). sample_submission.csv: the most-cited candidates everywhere.
query_id identifies a citing work (opaque). candidate_id identifies a cited work and carries no information about subject/age/identity except through training references. title and abstract are the query's own text (abstract reconstructed from an inverted index, plain text). year 2015-2024. field = broad field of primary topic, empty where none. References per query inside the pool: median 5, mean 6.7. No work is both a query and a candidate. A candidate joins the pool at five citations within the source corpus.
Source: OpenAlex snapshot June 2026 (CC0). English-language journal articles 2015-2024 with an abstract and at least ten references. OpenAlex ids, DOIs and URLs not published; identifiers regenerated with a salted hash after the split. Author information not retained, so self-citations remain and are not marked.

## Constraints
One NVIDIA A10G; the full script from reading ./dataset/public/ to writing ./working/submission.csv within 90 minutes. No network access. Generally pretrained text encoders are allowed when available offline and licensed for competition use; fine-tuning them on the training queries is allowed. Do not attempt to identify the underlying works or match published queries against OpenAlex, Crossref, a search engine or any other copy of the literature; recovering a query's real reference list by external lookup is not permitted. Each query is answered on its own: ranking candidates for one query uses that query's text and whatever was learned from the training queries; it does not use the other test queries collectively. Do not fit, calibrate or select against the test queries as a set, do not pool statistics over them, and do not derive labels from them and train on those.

## Submission
submission.csv with exactly query_id (string) and candidate_ids (string, up to 50 candidate identifiers separated by single spaces, best first). Exactly 10,106 rows plus header, one per query_id in test_queries.parquet, no duplicates. Repeated candidates in a row ignored after first appearance; identifiers outside the pool never match. Missing, duplicate or unknown query rows are rejected.
