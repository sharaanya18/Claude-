# U8 — Code-switched speech linking (user-supplied, verbatim minus site chrome; Domain: RAG, Medium, A10G, tag text)

## Overview
In a bilingual legislature, members speak Basque, Spanish, or both. Many switch language in the middle of a speech. You are given one Spanish paragraph from such a speech. You are also given a gallery of Basque paragraphs, each taken from a different code-switched speech delivered in the same fortnight of plenary sittings, on the same agenda, often by the same people. Exactly one of them comes from the same speech as the Spanish paragraph. Which one?
The records are real transcriptions of parliamentary plenary sittings between 2012 and 2020. What ties the two paragraphs together is not a translation and not a shared keyword. It is one speaker carrying one argument across a language switch: the same position, the same opponent, the same line of reasoning, the same rhetorical habits, now expressed in the other language. Everything else in the gallery is about the same political week, and in three quarters of cases the gallery also holds another speech by the very same speaker.

## Task definition
Cross-lingual retrieval of discourse continuity in code-switched speech. A case is one Spanish query paragraph and one gallery of Basque candidate paragraphs. Return the gallery's paragraph identifiers ranked, most likely first. Exactly one is correct. A gallery is built from the code-switched speeches of a two-week window of sittings; exactly one Basque paragraph from each speech, chosen at random within the speech, so the correct candidate is not systematically the paragraph next to the query. Every query of a gallery ranks the same gallery. Evaluation galleries hold 27 to 71 candidates (50 at median). Queries are drawn for about four in five of the gallery's speeches, one Spanish paragraph each, so every candidate is the correct answer for at most one query and about one in five candidates is correct for none. Prediction unit: one ranking per query, produced independently for each of 1,791 evaluation queries; 4,287 training queries with answers.
Difference from neighbours: translation retrieval/bitext mining pairs a text with its translation (here the paragraphs say different things; a translation-matching model finds the paragraph most similar in content, usually a different speaker on the same topic). Cross-language authorship attribution asks who wrote a text; here in 75.5% of evaluation cases another speech by the same speaker sits in the gallery as a decoy, so knowing the speaker does not resolve the case. The target is the speech.

## Inputs
For every paragraph its text and language (eu Basque, es Spanish). Paragraphs are 120 to 1,500 characters (274 median). Stage directions, bracketed names, every digit and capitalised name sequences inside sentences were removed. Procedural contributions by the chair excluded. No speaker, party, gender, date, speech/paragraph position or sitting identity. Every identifier is an opaque random code. Candidates of each query are listed in a seeded shuffle drawn per query; the correct candidate stands first 2.0% of the time vs 2.05% by chance.

## Supervision and split
Split holds out TIME and SITTINGS together along the boundary between two legislative terms. Training cases: first term (Dec 2012 - Jul 2016). Evaluation: second term (Oct 2016 - Feb 2020). No sitting, speech, paragraph or gallery on both sides. Political agendas, governments, ministers and party positions change between the terms, so topical associations from the first term do not carry over directly. Speakers partly continue: 36 of the 84 speakers in evaluation galleries also speak in training. Galleries are built separately on each side, so no candidate is shared between training and evaluation.

## Evaluation
Mean reciprocal rank over 1,791 queries (query omitted correct paragraph scores 0). Random 0.0913; supplied order 0.0908; text-ignoring rankings 0.0852-0.0963 (identifier sort 0.0920, reversed 0.0879, candidate frequency across galleries 0.0945, never-named-in-training 0.0852, longest first 0.0946, length-closeness 0.0963). Without training: word TF-IDF 0.1305; character 3-5-gram TF-IDF 0.2067; frozen public multilingual sentence encoders 0.2167 and 0.2701 (the strongest no-training route).

## Dataset (public/)
paragraphs.csv (10,514 rows: paragraph_id, language, text); train.csv (4,287 rows: query_id, gallery_id, query_paragraph_id, candidate_paragraph_ids space-separated in a per-query shuffled order); test.csv (1,791 rows, same columns); train_labels.csv (query_id, same_speech_paragraph_id); sample_submission.csv (query_id, ranked_paragraph_ids; supplied order, 0.0908).
Submission: CSV query_id,ranked_paragraph_ids, one row per test query (1,791 + header), any order. Identifiers not candidates of that query are ignored, repeated identifier counts once at first position, empty cell scores zero, space or comma separators; duplicated query_id, missing column, or no shared query_id is rejected.

## Rules
Only valid input signal: the supplied paragraph text and language, and what is learned from training queries and answers. Not allowed: hardcoding rankings for specific evaluation queries; using query_id/paragraph_id/gallery_id strings as signal; using row order or the order of identifiers within candidate_paragraph_ids; using how often a candidate appears across evaluation galleries; reasoning across evaluation queries (using the fact that each candidate answers at most one query, or reallocating candidates between queries of the same gallery - each ranking must be produced from its own query paragraph and its own candidates); looking up the paragraphs in any outside copy of the proceedings (session records, redistributed corpora, news, subtitles, search services); translating with an external translation service or API; private, role-gated or API-key models or external inference APIs; non-reproducible external weights not publicly available.
Pretrained model policy: fine-tuning a publicly available pretrained multilingual text model or sentence encoder is permitted provided the fitting genuinely uses the supplied training queries and answers. Knowledge a pretrained model carries about the two languages and about politics is legitimate; recognising a specific published speech is the prohibited outside lookup.
Compute: A10G.
